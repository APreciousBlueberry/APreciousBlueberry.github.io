Quick pitch

My edit of For All Mankind. I restructured the show's first two seasons (20-hrs of TV) as three cinematic and self-contained films (6-hr trilogy) each with its own satisfying ending. Red Moon follows Ed and Molly as NASA responds to losing the space race and searches for lunar ice. Into the Abyss traps Ed alone on the Moon as NASA mounts a dangerous rescue mission. And A City Upon a Hill concludes the trilogy with an armed conflict on the Moon and a focus on Gordo and Tracy.

Details

It's 1969 and the beginning of an alternate history as the Soviet Union lands the first man on the Moon, escalating the space race into a new race to establish the first permanent base on the Moon.

In this TV-to-film fan-edit of For All Mankind, I edited the first two seasons — the moon seasons — into a trilogy of films. Those moon seasons have three distinct stories separated by two time jumps. The show's first five episodes features Ed and Molly as they react to losing the moon race, train women astronauts, and travel to the moon in a search for ice, and that became the basis for the first movie, Red Moon. Then time jump, and the show's next five episodes features primarily Ed where he is stranded and alone on the moon, and meanwhile NASA launches a dangerous mission to rescue him, and that became the basis for the second movie, Red Moon 2 Into the Abyss. Then time jump, and the show's next ten episodes (all of season two) features an armed conflict on the moon and gestures of peace at home, and that became the basis for the third and final movie, Red Moon 3 A City Upon a Hill. Many characters play important roles, but in this third movie I treat Gordo and Tracy as the main characters.

Telling a story in a movie format instead of a TV format means important structural differences. TV episodes frequently split setups and payoffs across different episodes. Each episode usually ends on some kind of "to be continued" cliffhanger, and the next episode will jump right in and assume that you have the required "previously on" knowledge. The goal of the TV format is to make you feel like you must watch the next episode. But a movie, on the other hand, is expected to be more self-contained. Setups and payoffs are paired together and frame the whole story. We expect movies to finish with a satisfying ending, like turning the last page of a good book, and that feeling is what I strived for with each movie in this fan-edited trilogy.

I don't profit from this fan-edit in any way, and this edit should not be used as a substitute for purchasing the original work. To watch this edit, you're expected to already have legal access to the original show, such as on blu-ray or through a streaming service.

Red Moon (1h59m)

It's 1969 and the beginning of an alternate history. NASA is in crisis as the Soviet Union lands the first man on the Moon. Meanwhile, Deke must recruit female astronauts after the Soviets land a woman on the Moon. Also, Ed and the crew change Apollo 15's landing site after lunar ice is detected.

Sources: Red Moon, He Built the Saturn V, Nixon's Women, Prime Crew, Into the Abyss

Red Moon 2 Into the Abyss (1h49m)

Ed, Gordo, and Dani struggle with an extended Jamestown mission. Meanwhile, NASA preps a relief mission, but a crisis in space puts the Apollo 24 and 25 crews in peril, while Karen receives devastating news. Also, Ed handles a trespasser, while Ellen and Deke chart a new course without mission control.

Sources: Hi Bob, Bent Bird, Rupture, A City Upon a Hill, with brief scenes from Home Again

Red Moon 3 A City Upon a Hill (2h42m)

Tracy attempts to adapt to life in space, and Gordo grapples with life on Earth. Meanwhile, a dispute on the Moon prompts NASA officials to consider arming astronauts, and NASA prepares for a new mission with unlikely partners. Tensions on the Moon between the U.S. and the Soviets reach an all-time high, throwing multiple missions into jeopardy.

Sources: Pathfinder, The Weight, Best Laid Plans, And Here's to You, Triage, The Grey, with brief scenes from Every Little Thing, The Bleeding Edge, Rules of Engagement, Don't Be Cruel
